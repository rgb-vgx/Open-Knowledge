So, a very real problem you need to be aware of is something called catastrophic forgetting
when you're fine-tuning a model.
If you don't take care during fine-tuning, you can end up making your model better at
something at the expense of everything else, and that's not a good thing.
So, you need to know some techniques for avoiding that.
As an example here, let's say that I have a really good model that has not been fine-tuned
yet and it's great at coding and some basic legal tasks and it can follow instructions
and it has good general reasoning capabilities as well.
A lot of this came maybe from post-training or stuff that was specifically in the training
data set, right?
But if I then try to take that general purpose model and do heavy fine-tuning for a specific
narrow field like legal expertise, for example, that might come at the cost of everything else.
So, if I do too much fine-tuning on that narrow legal data, I might end up degrading my capabilities
for reasoning, coding, instruction following, and other topics, and you don't want that.
Now, this is most likely to happen when the new data you're fine-tuning on is very narrow
in scope or very different from the original data.
So, if you have a model that's about something and you're trying to fine-tune it for something
else entirely, that's when this can become a big issue.
The way to fix this is to mix in general data, not just the data that you're fine-tuning on.
If you're fine-tuning an Amazon Nova model, they have something called data mixing that
is specifically for this purpose.
You can also just not be quite so aggressive on the fine-tuning.
So, do it with a modest learning rate.
Do it with a modest number of epochs.
Just try not to push it too hard in that specific direction.
Otherwise, you might lose those other capabilities and experience catastrophic forgetting.
And remember, when you're evaluating your new model, don't just evaluate it on the new task.
It's very tempting to say, I'm fine-tuning this model on legal expertise, and I'm going
to evaluate how good it is on legal expertise, but you also need to evaluate what it could
do before and make sure that you did not give that up in the process, assuming it's important to you.
So, don't just evaluate what you're training it on.
Also test on the general capabilities that you don't want to lose.
And an exam tip, make sure that when you're doing customization of models through fine-tuning
or anything else, you are improving the target task without breaking everything else.
When that happens, we call that catastrophic forgetting.
