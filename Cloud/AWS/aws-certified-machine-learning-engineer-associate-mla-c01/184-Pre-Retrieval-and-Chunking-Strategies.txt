A little bit more about chunking. So part of the idea of what we're trying to do is to make sure
we don't end up feeding more information into the LLM than it can handle. So by restricting how much
information we're storing, we're reducing the risk of passing too many tokens in the end because
our context just has too much stuff in it. So if you take this really simple, one way to do this
is to just take the input data and chunk it up into blocks of text that are within that token limit,
right? So make sure that you don't blow that limit when you're passing that on to an actual query.
And usually that's what people do. They just say, okay, I'm going to take chunks of a thousand
characters at a time or whatever it is. Maybe there's some overlap between chunks, but that's
a very naive way of going about chunking. And that's what things like lang chain do by default.
That limit is becoming larger and larger though. So it's becoming less of a constraint, but more
tokens do cost more money. So there's economic reasons to try to keep that under control as well.
Also, there's an idea of semantic chunking. So that says, okay, I'm going to chunk things up,
