An important thing in the world of agentic AI is Model Context Protocol, or MCP for short,
something Anthropic came up with a while ago, but it's really caught on. It's a standardized
interface for interactions between agents and tools, and the cool thing is that this enables
you to use external tools that are offered by third parties. So Anthropic described it originally
as a USB-C port for AI applications, so it's a standardized interface for getting context or
additional information or capabilities from external third-party systems potentially.
It doesn't have to be third-party, it could be your own as well, but it's just a standard
protocol for extending your AI agents with other stuff, you know, and what could that other stuff
be? So in this diagram on the right here, you can see that you have your AI application at the top,
it goes through the MCP layer, and that MCP protocol can front a bunch of other stuff.
Maybe it's access to data or file systems or databases locally, maybe it's development tools
locally, maybe it's productivity tools, there's tons of examples out there, but they could be
local, they could be external, I don't care, it all looks the same to MCP. Now technically the
data layer is JSON RPC 2.0, I don't think you'll have to know that, but just so you know, and the
transport layer is either standard IO for local stuff or HTTP streaming for external stuff.
So it just gives you access to external tools from your agentic AI system with a consistent
interaction pattern. So it's a standard on how apps provide context back to your foundation
model, to your LLM agentic system, and pretty much any agent SDK has model context protocol
support. MCP support is kind of a table stakes these days for these SDKs, and that
includes the strands SDK, so strands also has support for MCP baked into it. Okay, what do these servers
do? Well the way it works is that they can expose tools, resources, and prompts. So they might give
you back prompts on how to use them, and they might expose tools that you can use to extend
the capabilities of your system, or resources to provide additional context back to your system.
Okay, and just to make it real and kind of wrap your head around some examples here,
