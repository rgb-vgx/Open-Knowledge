Okay, so in the exam,
most likely you will have to choose
Kinesys data streams over Amazon MSK
because AWS likes you to use their products.
But it is important that maybe in one or two edge cases,
you will have to say
that Amazon MSK is the right answer.
And for you to know it, you need to know the differences
between Kinesis data streams
and Amazon MSK and the main ones.
So in Kinesis data streams,
there is a limit of 10 megabytes per message.
So in Amazon MSK, though, it's one megabyte by default,
but you can configure for higher, for example, 10 megabytes,
and that is going to be a huge difference.
So if you see a large message in the exam,
this is going to rule out any Kinesis data streams answers
or Firehose answers, and go straight into Amazon MSK.
Then in Kinesis data streams,
it's called data streams with shards.
And in Amazon MSK, it's called Kafka Topics with partitions,
but they're pretty similar.
In data streams though,
you can do shard splitting and merging,
so increase or decrease the throughput
of your data stream.
Whereas in Amazon MSK scaling is a little bit harder
and you can only add partitions to a topic.
You cannot remove partitions from a topic.
In terms of encryption,
Kinesis data streams has TLS in flight encryption
by default enabled.
Whereas for Amazon MSK, you have the option to choose
to send the data over plain text, so no encryption
or TLS in-flight encryption as well.
You get KMS at rest encryption for both services.
And in terms of security, you have IAM policies
for authorization and authentication
on the Kinesis data stream side.
And for security on the Amazon MSK,
you can get mutual TLS for authentication
and Kafka SCLs for authorization, that's combo number one.
Or SASL/SCRAM for authentication
and Kafka SCLs for authorization, that's number two.
Or IAM Access Control to do both authorization
and authentication within MSK as well.
So you have to choose one of these options.
