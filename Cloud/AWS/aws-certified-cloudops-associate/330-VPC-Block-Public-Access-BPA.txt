So now, let's talk
about VPC Block Public Access feature, or BPA.
It's a way for you to centrally block ingress
or egress internet access to VPCs and subnets.
What does that mean?
It means traffic that goes from the internet
into your account
or traffic that goes from your account to the internet.
So, why would you wanna do this?
Well, it helps you ensure compliance
and security requirements.
So you have two modes.
Bidirectional, in which you block all internet traffic
to and from the VPCs to ensure that no matter what,
you will never touch the internet.
Or ingress-only to block all traffic to or from the VPCs,
except for NAT gateways and egress-only internet gateways.
That means that the instances within your accounts
will be able to communicate with the internet, for example,
to pull updates or data,
but the internet will never be able to access
whatever is in your account.
Then, you also have the ability
to include single VPCs and subnets with two exclusion modes.
You have bidirectional to allow all internet traffic
to or from the excluded VPCs and subnets,
or egress-only to only allow outbound internet
from the excluded VPCs and subnets.
So it's just an exclusion mode on the two modes from before.
This can be enabled for each AWS Region
and you can verify if the traffic is blocked
using, for example, the VPC Reachability Analyzer tool.
So it's a great way for you to have a rule above all of them
to make sure you block public access
and you are very secure.
