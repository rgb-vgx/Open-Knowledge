these kind of recommendations.

and right-size your workload.

It's going to use machine learning
to analyze your resources' configuration,
and look at the utilization thanks to CloudWatch metrics.
So it supports the resources
and that's important for you to know.
EC2 instances, EC2 auto-scaling groups,
EBS volumes, Lambda functions,
ECS on Fargate, Aurora, and RDS databases,
as well as commercial software licenses.
Using Compute Optimizer can help you lower your cost
by up to 25%.
And the recommendations can be exported into Amazon S3.
So what else do you need to know?
Well, if you want to be able
to view the recommendations made by Compute Optimizer,
your users need access to one IAM AWS-managed policy
called ComputeOptimizerReadOnlyAccess.
Also, if your EC2 instances
do not appear in Compute Optimizer,
that may mean that your EC2 instance is actually new
and has insufficient metrics data.
The solution is to keep the instance running
for at least 30 hours to allow Compute Optimizer
to collect enough metrics data to generate recommendations,
and that can come up in the exam.
