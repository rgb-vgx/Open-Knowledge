So now let's talk about Route 53
Application Recovery Controller.
So it's a way for you to automate
and orchestrate application recovery
across availability zones and regions.
The idea is that it's a service
meant for mission-critical applications
that need high availability and minimal downtime.
So it's a way for you to manage multi-region active/standby
or active/active setups.
And it's necessary if you have an environment
with regulatory or business continuity requirements.
So how does that work? Well, you have readiness checks.
You're going to check continuously
to validate that the standby
and the replica infrastructure is ready.
And you have routing control and DNS integration.
So you have traffic switches to reroute users
between the different AZs and regions
via DNS and health checks.
You can also do a full zonal shift and region shift
to shift traffic away from impaired AZs
or orchestrate a full region-level failover.
So let me show you in a diagram.
So say you have a region that is active in us-east-1,
you have an application balancer,
EC2 instances across several AZs,
and DynamoDB in the background.
And say you have created a standby region in eu-west-2,
with, again, an ALB and the exact same architecture.
You're going to use Route 53,
you're going to create a recovery group
thanks to Application Recovery Controller.
And then you're going to create resource sets.
So one for your load balancers, one for your EC2 instances,
and one for DynamoDB.
And you're going to have readiness checks within.
And so whenever you're ready, you can fail over,
provided that the readiness checks are okay,
but Route 53 is going to be the overarching service
that's going to make sure that you can perform your failover
and your disaster recovery.
So it's the one service in AWS that can be helpful
for these kind of use cases.
